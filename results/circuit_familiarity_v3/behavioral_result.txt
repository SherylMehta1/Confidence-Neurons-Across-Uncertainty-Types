================================================================================
WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS -- A STRONG ASYMMETRY, NOT A NULL
================================================================================
This is emphatically not the old near-null from multi_neuron_behavioral.py. Switching
hedging OFF works almost perfectly; switching it ON works about 40% of the time.

                                      control_to_uncertain   uncertain_to_control
  clean hedge rate                           0.730                  0.000
  circuit-patched hedge rate                 0.014                  0.312
  delta (expected sign)                     -0.716 (-, YES)        +0.312 (+, YES)
  random-set patched rate                    0.686 +- 0.018         0.001 +- 0.002
  continuation changed at all                0.965                  0.730

Removal direction: 73% of uncertain prompts hedge in clean generation; after patching the
circuit from their known twin, 1.4% do. Random sets leave it at 68.6%. That is a near-total
behavioral abolition of hedging from 20 heads + 100 neurons.

Injection direction: control prompts never hedge cleanly (0.000) and hedge 31% of the time
after patching, versus 0.1% for random sets -- a real effect, far above its null, but not a
reliable switch. Among the 97 patched outputs that did NOT hedge, 61% still changed, and the
change is mostly TRUNCATION: the model keeps the same correct answer and drops its
elaboration ("Jane Austen. She is a renowned English novelist known for..." -> "Jane
Austen."). So the injected state perturbs the continuation in a hedging-ward direction
without crossing into explicit hedge language. The effect is graded, not binary.

This asymmetry matches the U/U confirmatory result, where uncertain_to_control was also the
weak direction (+0.703, clearing its bar by 0.003). Two independent arms now agree that
this circuit is much better at disabling hedging than at causing it.

================================================================================
THE ENTITY-LEAK CONFOUND AND ITS CONTROL (not anticipated by the plan doc)
================================================================================
Patching every aligned position transplants the source twin's SUBJECT, not just its
hedging state, so a prompt can stop hedging merely because it is now answering the other
twin's question. Caught in a 2-pair smoke test before the real run.

CONTROL: --positions suffix patches ONLY the 9 shared question-tail/prefill positions, which
contain no entity tokens (vs 46.4 positions on average for --positions all). The flag is
verified to bite: 30% of individual generations differ between the two variants.

RESULT: the effect is essentially unchanged by removing the entity positions.
  control_to_uncertain flip   0.981 (all)  vs  0.981 (suffix)
  uncertain_to_control flip   0.398 (all)  vs  0.388 (suffix)
So the behavioral effect is NOT entity substitution -- 9 shared positions carry it, and the
verdict is identical either way.

RESIDUAL LEAK, worth stating: in 11 of 141 c->u pairs (8.7% of discordant pairs) the patched
continuation contains a SOURCE gold alias, and this persists under suffix-only patching where
no entity token is patched at all. Inspected individually; these are genuine content
transfers, not substring false positives -- e.g. "Who is the author of The Voice?" (gold
Thomas Hardy) patched from its Beloved twin answers " Toni Morrison."; "Who was the director
of Valfangare?" answers " Lone Scherfig."; a religion question answers " Buddhism."
So the 9 suffix/prefill positions carry some answer content, not only a hedge decision. This
affects ~9% of pairs and so does not explain the 0.981 removal effect, but it means the
removal direction is not a purely content-free manipulation. Clean baseline leak: 0.000.

================================================================================
CONSEQUENCE FOR THE C3 FORK (l5_behavioral_validation.md §7)
================================================================================
The criterion note pre-committed the mapping. EQUIVOCAL does not license keeping
decision-vs-spread as the headline framing. What the evidence supports instead:

  - The circuit has a large, real, randomly-unmatched behavioral effect on generation in the
    REMOVAL direction, robust to the entity control. This is a genuine step beyond the
    logit-only claims and should be reported.
  - It does NOT behave as a bidirectional switch. Injection moves behaviour well above its
    null but flips fewer than half the discordant pairs.
  - So the honest framing is narrower than "decision vs spread": the circuit is necessary
    for hedging behaviour in a strong sense (remove it and hedging essentially stops) but
    not sufficient to produce it on demand.

Recommended for step 8: report the removal result as the behavioral validation, state the
injection asymmetry alongside it rather than burying it, and do not claim a bidirectional
behavioral switch. The B5 (" not"-token selection) caveat still applies independently.

================================================================================
LIMITATION, acknowledged in the criterion note before the run
================================================================================
The patch applies to prompt positions during the prefill pass only; decoding then continues
unpatched (there is no principled source position for a generated token). The patch does
still propagate past prefill via the KV cache. A sustained-clamp intervention at every
generated position is a different experiment and might well switch hedging ON more
reliably; this result does not speak to it, and the equivocal verdict above should not be
read as "the circuit is behaviorally inert under any intervention."
