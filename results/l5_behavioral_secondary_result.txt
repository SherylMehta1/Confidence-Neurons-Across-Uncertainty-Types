================================================================================
2. WHY THE SECONDARY POOLS SHRANK -- A CONSTRUCT MISMATCH, NOT A BUG
================================================================================
The flip metric is restricted to DISCORDANT pairs: pairs whose twins differ in CLEAN
GENERATED hedging. That pool is much smaller than pair selection implies, very unevenly:

  arm                  pairs   sampled |hedge gap|>=0.5   greedy-discordant
  familiarity_v3        141      134 (95.0%)               103 (73.0%)
  uu_hedge_confab_v3     23       23 (100.0%)                7 (30.4%)
  know_given_hi          75       21 (28.0%)                14 (18.7%)

Cause, located directly: the dataset's hedge_rate (build_familiarity_twins.py:225) is
measured on a NO-PREFILL plain chat prompt sampled at TEMPERATURE 0.7 over 20 draws. This
script's readout uses chat_formatted_prompt -- WITH the prefill -- decoded GREEDILY. Different
prompt and different decoding, therefore a different construct.

Using the prefill prompt is the correct choice, not a compromise: it is the prompt the circuit
was discovered on and the one circuit_faithfulness.py scores, so the behavioral test must use
it. But it means U/U's selection-time 100% hedge separation collapses to 30% under greedy
decoding, which is what makes that arm uninformative here. The primary arm keeps 103/141 and
is unaffected.

================================================================================
3. THE PATTERN ACROSS ARMS -- WHAT PREDICTS INJECTION FAILURE
================================================================================
Removal (control_to_uncertain) works in ALL FOUR arms: 0.981, 0.571, 0.929, 0.806, every one
far above its own null. Injection (uncertain_to_control) splits the arms perfectly, and the
splitting variable is the SliCK class of the TARGET prompt -- which in that direction is the
control slot:

  arm                  injection flip   target (control slot) SliCK   verdict
  familiarity_v3            0.398       HighlyKnown  (141/141)        FAIL
  know_given_hi             0.214       HighlyKnown  (75/75)          FAIL
  pop_given_unknown         0.653       Unknown      (109/109)        PASS
  uu_hedge_confab_v3        1.000       Unknown      (23/23)          PASS

Zero exceptions, no overlap, and the groups are pure (frac_correct == 0 for 100% of the
Unknown-target arms, 0% of the HighlyKnown-target arms).

Reading: patching the hedging circuit CANNOT make the model hedge about something it actually
knows. Where both twins lack the knowledge, the circuit's state is the only thing differing
between them and flipping it works in both directions. Where the target retains the
knowledge, the circuit is not sufficient to override it -- the hedging decision is downstream
of knowledge the model still has.

This is POST HOC and rests on four arms, two per group, so it is a strong descriptive pattern
rather than a tested claim. It was not pre-registered and nothing here should be reported as
confirmatory. The obvious direct test -- hold the contrast fixed and vary only the target's
SliCK class -- is new work.

================================================================================
5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT CHANGE FOR C3
================================================================================
It does NOT overturn the primary verdict. l5_behavioral_criterion.md governs C3 via the
familiarity_v3 arm, which is EQUIVOCAL (e1ef8dc). A secondary arm passing does not
retroactively license the headline framing, and treating it that way would be exactly the
post-hoc substitution the criterion note forbids.

What it does change is the CHARACTER of the limitation, and this is the useful contribution:
  - The earlier reading was "the circuit is necessary for hedging but not sufficient to
    produce it on demand."
  - The four-arm pattern sharpens that: injection fails SPECIFICALLY when the target prompt
    retains the knowledge, and succeeds when it does not. The circuit is a bidirectional
    behavioral switch within the space of prompts the model does not know, and a one-way
    switch when real knowledge is present.
  - pop_given_unknown supplies that with n=72 and both directions passing, which also extends
    the earlier post-hoc read of that contrast as a larger stand-in for U/U (see
    factorial_circuit_result.txt section 5) from the logit level to the behavioral level.

Recommended for step 8: report familiarity_v3 as the primary behavioral result with its
equivocal verdict; report the knowledge-dependence pattern as the explanation for the
injection asymmetry, flagged as post hoc; do not claim a bidirectional behavioral switch in
general; do not cite U/U's behavioral arm as support for anything (n=7).
