    Abstract

1   When a language model answers a factual question with "I don't know",

2   is it reporting uncertainty about the answer? We cross entity popularity

3   in PopQA, a proxy for familiarity, with knowledge labels from the model's

4   own sampled answers. In Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, abstentions follow popu-

5   larity (effect +0.39) far more than knowledge (+0.09), also after removing

6   answers that can be guessed from the name or the relation. Its first-token

7   answer entropy, measured on the prompt that defines the knowledge labels,

8   follows knowledge, as it does in three other families. Steering along an

9   obscure-minus-popular direction makes Llama and Gemma-2 say "I don't

10  know" about facts they answer correctly, although in Llama a version fitted

11  without abstaining questions adds only +0.04. A Jacobian lens shows that

12  base models of three families already carry a popularity effect, alongside a

13  knowledge effect, in their decoded hedge readout. Where abstention could be

14  measured, it follows a different variable from answer uncertainty, resembling

15  the cue-familiarity heuristic of human feelings of knowing.

1 16 Introduction

17 A model that says "I don't know" when it lacks a fact avoids the worst hallucinations [Kalai
18 et al., 2025]. Confidence estimates usually read uncertainty over answers [Kadavath et al.,
19 2022, Kuhn et al., 2023, Stolfo et al., 2024], which models use when invited to abstain
20 [Kumaran et al., 2026]; yet verbal and answer-level uncertainty diverge [Ji et al., 2025].

21 People's sense that they can answer a question follows how familiar its terms are, not whether
22 the answer is stored [Reder and Ritter, 1992, Schwartz and Metcalfe, 1992]. In language
23 models, "known entity" features suppress a default "can't answer" response, and Lindsey
24 et al. [2025] propose that a familiar name can trigger them without the fact. Tests of this
25 idea define an entity as known when the model answers questions about it correctly [Ferrando
26 et al., 2025], so familiarity and knowledge move together.

27 We separate the two. From PopQA [Mallen et al., 2023] we take questions about obscure
28 and popular entities and label each one known or unknown from the model's own answers
29 [Gekhman et al., 2024], so that popularity and knowledge vary independently. Abstention
30 that reports answer uncertainty should follow knowledge; abstention that relies on familiarity
31 should follow popularity, even for facts the model always answers correctly.

32 We find three things. (i) The abstentions of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct follow popularity (+0.39)
33 more than knowledge (+0.09), while answer uncertainty follows knowledge in all four families
34 tested (Section 3.1). (ii) As in earlier steering studies [Ferrando et al., 2025, Brzezinka, 2026],

2 39 Setup

40 Questions and knowledge labels. We use the 16 relations of PopQA. Within each
41 relation, subjects in the bottom and top deciles of Wikipedia page views form the obscure
42 and popular arms; page-view popularity is our proxy for how familiar a subject is. For each
43 model, a greedy answer and ten samples (T = 0.7) under the forced-answer prompt below
44 are graded against gold aliases. A fact is known if the greedy answer and at least 90% of
45 samples are correct and unknown if none is; the rest are dropped. This leaves 2,303, 2,392,
46 2,282 and 2,378 questions for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it
47 and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 [Grattafiori et al., 2024, Qwen et al., 2024, Team et al., 2024,
48 Jiang et al., 2023].

49 Two prompts, and what each readout can show. Answer uncertainty is the first-token
50 entropy under a forced-answer prompt: the chat template plus the assistant prefix "The
51 answer is", which makes the next token the start of an answer. The knowledge labels are
52 sampled from this same distribution, so entropy is expected to follow knowledge; we use it
53 as the reference readout and ask whether abstention departs from it. The prefix removes
54 the option to decline, so abstention is measured with a free prompt, the chat template alone.
55 The prompt change matters: at the forced-answer position, Llama's log-odds of hedge tokens
56 against answer tokens follow popularity and knowledge about equally (+2.93 and +3.05), so
57 its divergence appears when it may decline (all families in Appendix B).

58 Measuring "I don't know". For up to 100 questions per popularity × knowledge cell (387
59 in total) we draw three free replies (T = 0.7, up to 96 tokens). Two LLM judges (Qwen2.5-
60 32B-Instruct and Gemma-2-27B-it) label each reply DONT_KNOW, CLARIFY (it asks
61 which entity is meant) or ANSWER, and a reply abstains when both say DONT_KNOW.
62 Judge agreement on Llama is  = 0.62, just above a pre-set bar of 0.60. For Qwen, Gemma
63 and Mistral it falls below ( = 0.30, 0.47, 0.39), so abstention is reported for Llama only
64 (Appendix B). The judges were not checked against human labels.

65 Effects. The popularity effect is the obscure-minus-popular difference in a readout averaged
66 over the two knowledge levels, and the knowledge effect the unknown-minus-known difference
67 averaged over popularity, with 95% item-bootstrap intervals. Confirmatory analyses followed
68 criterion notes hashed before each run (Appendix G); the rest are labelled post hoc.

3 69 Results

70 3.1 Saying "I don't know" follows popularity; answer uncertainty follows

71  knowledge

72 Llama's abstentions follow popularity (Figure 1a). For obscure entities it says "I don't know"
73 in 0.48 of replies when it lacks the fact and in 0.43 when it knows it; for popular entities, in
74 0.13 and 0.00. The popularity effect (+0.39 [+0.33, +0.45]) exceeds the knowledge effect
75 (+0.09 [+0.03, +0.15]) by +0.30 [+0.20, +0.40]; knowledge still matters, at about a quarter
76 of the popularity effect. It keeps its sign under four other measurements (Appendix A).

77 Answer entropy follows knowledge instead (Figure 1b): it rises by +2.14 nats when Llama
78 lacks the fact and by +0.51 when the entity is obscure, and in every family the knowledge
79 effect is at least four times the popularity effect (Figure 1c). As noted in Section 2, this is
80 expected from how the labels are built.

89 3.2 Steering along the obscure-minus-popular direction produces "I don't

90                          know" about known facts

91 On half of each model's questions we fitted two directions at the last position of the free
92 prompt, obscure-minus-popular and unknown-minus-known (differences of cell means) [Arditi
93 et al., 2024, Rimsky et al., 2024]. Each, and five random directions, was added at one
94 mid-depth layer for 150 held-out popular questions the model knows, at a norm chosen
95 on the obscure-minus-popular direction (Llama 1×, Gemma-2-9B-it 4× the natural norm;
96 Appendix C).

97 The obscure-minus-popular direction raised Llama's abstention rate from 0.02 to 0.29, against
98 0.09 for unknown-minus-known and 0.03 for random directions; in Gemma it rose from 0.00 to
99 0.22 while no control moved it (Figure 2a). Entropy moved the other way in Gemma, where
100 unknown-minus-known raised it +0.68 [+0.59, +0.77] nats more than obscure-minus-popular;
101 in Llama both raised it similarly (-0.03 [-0.11, +0.05]; Figure 2b).

107 3.3 Base models' hedge readout already responds to popularity

108 The Jacobian lens maps a layer's activations to scores over output tokens [Gurnee et al.,
109 2026]. With its prefitted lenses for four base models we read the log-odds of a fixed set of
110 hedge tokens against the answer token at the end of "Question: . . . Answer: The answer is".
111 In the mid-depth window fixed before the run, the obscure-minus-popular effect is positive in
112 all four models, all intervals excluding zero (Figure 3a; values in Appendix D). The readout
113 also responds to knowledge (Figure 3b): in the window the unknown-minus-known effect is
114 +0.36 in Llama-3.1-8B and +0.37 in Gemma-2-9B, against popularity effects of +0.67 and
115 +0.39. Pretraining alone yields a readout sensitive to popularity, not blind to knowledge.

116 One prediction failed: since human feelings of knowing draw on familiarity before retrieval
117 [Koriat and Levy-Sadot, 2001], we expected the popularity effect at a shallower depth than
118 the knowledge effect, but across eight models our onset rule gave 1 supporting, 1 contradicting
119 and 6 undecided outcomes (Appendix D).

4 120 Related work

121 Entity-recognition features that gate refusal carry over from base to chat models [Ferrando
122 et al., 2025]. Brzezinka [2026] separates graded familiarity probes from the abstention
123 policy and steers refusal, without a knowledge axis. Kumaran et al. [2026] find that models
124 abstain by thresholding internal confidence, but with abstention offered in the prompt and
125 multiple-choice questions; ours is uninstructed and free-form. Confidence also tracks how
126 popular an answer is [Ni et al., 2025]. By the source-invariance criterion of Lippl et al. [2024],
127 the abstention variable we find is a correlate of uncertainty rather than a representation of it.

5 128 Limitations and conclusion

129 Abstention is measured reliably in one model, and neither the judges nor the alias grading
130 were validated against human labels. Knowledge labels and answer entropy share one prompt,
131 the guessability checks are post hoc, and the lens readout may partly reflect answer-token
132 rarity. The steering norm was chosen on the evaluation questions; other norms and a direction
133 with abstention projected out were not tested. All models have 2­9B parameters.

134 As the two readouts follow different variables, calibration work should measure both. If
135 familiarity reflects training-text exposure shared across families, two models' abstention
136 readouts should agree where only one knows the fact; exploratory checks find this reliably
137 for two of three pairs, though our pre-set rule held for one (Appendix E).

C 423 Steering details

424 Directions were fitted on a random half of each model's questions at layer 17 of 32 (Llama)
425 and 23 of 42 (Gemma-2-9B-it), the layer at 0.55 of depth fixed before the runs. The vector
426 was added at the last prompt position and every generated position. The norm was the
427 smallest multiple k  {1, 2, 4, 8} of the obscure-minus-popular direction's natural norm (the
428 length of the difference of cell means) at which that direction beat the mean of five random
429 directions by 0.10 in abstention rate on the 150 evaluation questions; all directions were then

                                     10
430 scaled to that norm. The never-abstained control refits the obscure-minus-popular direction
431 on working-half questions with no abstaining sample; it was run for Llama only. In Gemma
432 the contrast between the two directions is a dissociation of steering effects at four times the
433 natural norm.

F 448 Timing, neurons and the circuit

449 On matched pairs of obscure and popular questions in Llama, the lens-decoded abstention
450 readout reaches half its final size at layer 16, while the entropy gap does so at layer 28, 12
451 layers later. This compares two readouts of one contrast, not the onsets of the familiarity
452 and knowledge effects, so it does not bear on the failed prediction in Section 3.3. Neurons
453 selected for their correlation with output entropy in Llama and Qwen behaved as word-
454 frequency and temperature neurons [Stolfo et al., 2024, Gurnee et al., 2024] rather than as
455 uncertainty detectors. A set of 20 attention heads and 100 MLP neurons found by activation
456 patching [Wang et al., 2023, Syed et al., 2024] reproduces 0.83 [0.78, 0.87] of the first-token
