The headline dissociation comes from how "knowledge" is defined, not from anything the model does. A fact is "known" when Llama's greedy answer and at least 90% of ten samples under the forced-answer prompt are correct (lines 43-45). Answer uncertainty is the first-token entropy of that same distribution, which the authors admit makes it follow knowledge "by construction" (lines 51-53, 79-80). Half of the title is therefore a tautology. The other half rests on reading "known" as "stored". For obscure entities, a consistent forced answer is also what a confident guess looks like: nationality, religion, language or sport inferred from a name, or a common answer to the relation. The post hoc guessability filter catches only aliases that appear in the name and modal answers covering at least 20% of a relation (lines 405-407), and it already removes 30 of the 87 obscure "known" facts (lines 82-83). On the remaining 57, saying "I don't know" in a free reply is equally well explained as an accurate report that no fact is stored, which is the opposite of the paper's conclusion. No independent test of knowledge is offered, such as paraphrased prompts or related facts. This deciding cell comes from one model and two LLM judges at κ = 0.62 that were never checked against human labels (lines 62-64), and alternative measurements put the effect anywhere from +0.13 to +0.44 (Table 1). The cue-familiarity claim is not established.
