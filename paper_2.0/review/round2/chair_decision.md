### Decision: ACCEPT

Dear authors,

The panel agrees on the core of this revision. It is a clean 2x2 design that crosses PopQA page-view popularity with knowledge labels taken from the model's own samples. In Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it shows "I don't know" on 0.43 of free replies about obscure facts the model answers correctly under the forced-answer prompt, against 0.00 for popular ones. In that known arm, forced-answer entropy barely differs between obscure and popular entities. The effect also survives every check you report:
- five measurements, including a non-LLM detector;
- a guessability filter (+0.30 [+0.19, +0.41]);
- a popularity gap of +0.34 within unknown facts;
- a relation-fixed-effects regression.

The most conservative estimate, +0.13 [+0.03, +0.24], still excludes zero. The paper is also unusually candid: it applies a pre-set judge-reliability bar and keeps its failed prediction, its below-bar circuit result and a steering control that undercuts its own effect. That is the kind of early-stage and partly negative finding the UniReps call invites. No referee and no part of the red-team review identifies a flaw that alone would justify rejection.

What remains wrong is mainly that the title, abstract, figure titles and conclusion claim more than the body shows:
- entropy following knowledge is presented as a finding when your own labelling guarantees it (l.51-53, l.79-80);
- "familiarity" is a page-view proxy that is not separated from how much the model knows about the entity;
- the steering ranking and its "mostly through components shared with abstention" decomposition are not supported by the controls you ran;
- the steering result for Gemma rests on a detector you do not trust for Gemma;
- the base-model lens result is read at the forced-answer position, has a knowledge effect that is negative for OLMo inside the window, and may be partly an answer-token artefact.

These must all be reworded at camera-ready, as listed under Conditions below. The methods referee's evidential concerns limit how far the result can be interpreted. They do not show that the operational finding is wrong, so they must be stated plainly. The cheap analyses on existing data that would settle several of them are strongly encouraged:
- the accuracy of free replies in the obscure-known cell;
- relation-stratified effects;
- splitting the lens readout into its hedge-token and answer-token terms.

### Referee recommendations as given

No referee recommends rejection.

- **Methods: major revision.** The referee would support acceptance once F1-F3 are addressed (free-reply accuracy, an item-level uncertainty model, relation stratification) and claims (ii) and (iii) are softened. They describe the one-model behavioural result as "supported at workshop standard".
- **Framing: major revision.** Most fixes are rewording (title, contribution list, §3.3, a UniReps paragraph), and only F1 (entity-level knowledge) needs new analysis. "With those changes this would be a clear accept for the workshop."
- **Clarity: minor revision.** The referee would move to major if free-prompt entropy turns out to follow popularity (their Q1). That analysis has not been reported.
- **Red team:** the attack memo concludes that "the cue-familiarity claim is not established" and that the dissociation "comes from how 'knowledge' is defined". It makes no formal recommendation. The adjudication finds the memo overreaches on both of its strongest claims. It expects acceptance as a workshop abstract "only if the title and abstract are narrowed".

The venue allows only ACCEPT or REJECT. I did not average these recommendations. The decision above rests on the evidence.

### Disagreements

**D1. Recommendation level: methods and framing say major revision; clarity says minor revision.**
- *Methods:* F1-F4 are threats to the evidence for the headline cell: forced-prompt labels, the confound with entropy in the unknown arm, relation composition and no human validation.
- *Framing and clarity:* what drives their recommendations is mostly wording in the title, abstract, figure titles and §3.3.
- *Deciding passages:* Fig. 1a-b (known arm: 0.43 vs 0.00 abstention at about 0.95 vs 0.85 nats entropy); Table 1 (all five measurements positive, the lowest +0.13 [+0.03, +0.24]); l.83-84 (+0.30 after guessability filtering); l.85-86 and l.414-415 (popularity coefficient with relation fixed effects).
- *What the evidence favours:* the manuscript's evidence supports the operational finding against each of the methods referee's alternatives. Those alternatives bound how the finding can be interpreted ("known" means forced-prompt correct; "familiarity" means a popularity proxy), and none of them shows the finding is wrong.
- The methods referee also says acceptance follows from analyses on existing outputs plus softening. That makes this a disagreement about how much must be done before acceptance, not about whether the result is real.

**D2. What the never-abstained control shows: Fr F3 and Cl M4 vs Me F7.**
- *Framing and clarity:* they read +0.04 as showing that the effect runs mostly through abstention. Clarity suggests the wording "largely because the direction contains the difference between abstaining and answering".
- *Methods:* the refit selects on the outcome, removing the least-familiar obscure items along with the abstention content. Both accounts therefore predict a small residual.
- *Deciding passages:* App. C l.430-431 ("refits ... on working-half questions with no abstaining sample"); l.104-105. The size of the refit sample is not reported.
- *What the evidence favours:* methods, as in round-1 D2. The control rules out a large effect that survives removing abstaining items. It can support neither a familiarity-specific causal claim nor the decomposition "mostly through abstention components".
- Clarity's proposed wording is itself an untested claim about what the direction contains. The l.133 projection test would decide it.

**D3. What the unknown-arm popularity gap (+0.34) shows: Adj P2 vs Me F2.**
- *Adjudication:* the gap refutes the memo's "abstention accurately reports that nothing is stored" alternative, because that alternative predicts equal abstention for popular-unknown and obscure-unknown items.
- *Methods:* popular-unknown items have lower entropy (about 2.6 vs 3.5 nats), so an answer-uncertainty account also predicts the gap.
- *Deciding passages:* l.407-408 (which claims only that "mislabelled guesses cannot produce" it); Fig. 1b.
- *What the evidence favours:* both, against different alternatives. The gap rules out guess mislabelling, but not an uncertainty account. The comparison that bears on "a different variable from answer uncertainty" is the known arm, where entropy is nearly flat (Chair note N1). This shapes camera-ready item 2.

**D4. What the forced-prompt hedge numbers (+2.93 vs +3.05) mean: Fr F2/F4 vs Cl M2.**
- *Framing:* on the shared prompt the hedge readout does not dissociate, so the title's contrast is partly a change of prompt, and the forced-position lens cannot bear on abstention.
- *Clarity:* on the same prompt, the hedge readout weighs popularity and knowledge equally while entropy weighs knowledge about four times as much, so some of the readout difference exists within one prompt.
- *Deciding passages:* l.55-57; Table 2; l.77-79.
- *What the evidence favours:* the two readings are compatible, and each is right about one thing. Within the forced prompt, the ratio of popularity to knowledge is about 0.96 for the hedge readout and about 0.24 for entropy, so the readout differs. But the reversal, where popularity clearly exceeds knowledge, appears only in free-prompt behaviour, so the change of prompt matters. Framing's consequence for §3.3 follows (J16): a forced-position base-model readout can show sensitivity to both variables, not an inherited abstention bias.

**D5. Severity of individual issues: framing minor vs methods and clarity major (J14 Gemma steering; J17 OLMo knowledge sign).**
- *Deciding passages:* abstract l.8-10 and l.11-13, where both claims appear.
- *What the evidence favours:* major in both cases, because each is an abstract-level claim resting on a measurement the paper elsewhere disowns (J14) or that a figure contradicts for one of three families (J17). The fixes are still rewording.

**D6. Red-team memo vs adjudication on whether the dissociation comes "from how knowledge is defined".**
- *Memo:* it is definitional, and the cue-familiarity claim is not established.
- *Adjudication:* the abstention half is measured on a different prompt by judges independent of the knowledge labels. Its "by construction" quotation is the memo's own wording, not the authors'.
- *Deciding passages:* l.52 (the paper's text is "expected to follow knowledge"), l.54 and l.58-61.
- *What the evidence favours:* the adjudication, on the abstention half. The memo is right that the entropy half is expected from the labelling (J1) and that the cue-familiarity reading is an interpretation (J3).

**D7. How low is the regex's precision: "evidently low" (Me F6) vs "unknown" (Fr F11).**
- Table 2 lets one estimate it; see Chair note N2. That estimate favours methods for both Llama and Gemma, with the caveat given there.
