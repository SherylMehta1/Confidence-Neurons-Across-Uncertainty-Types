# Referee report: clarity and fresh-reader lens

**Manuscript:** "In Llama, Saying 'I Don't Know' Tracks Familiarity While Answer Uncertainty Tracks Knowledge" (UniReps 2026, extended abstract)
**Reviewer lens:** clarity for a non-specialist ML reader. I read only the manuscript (PDF, text copy and the three figure PNGs).

## What a skim leaves a reader believing, and whether the body agrees

A reader who skims the title, abstract, figure titles and conclusion would come away believing five things:
1. Llama's "I don't know" follows entity popularity, not knowledge.
2. Answer uncertainty follows knowledge in four families, as an independent finding.
3. A familiarity direction causes the model to abstain on facts it knows.
4. Base models already encode popularity in their hedging.
5. Calibration work should measure both readouts.

Only (1) and (5) match the body.

- **(2):** The body says this result is expected by construction (l.51-53, l.79-80).
- **(3):** The body says the direction "can produce abstention, not that the model uses familiarity" (l.102). A direction fitted without abstaining questions adds only +0.04.
- **(4):** The lens readout is taken on a forced-answer-style prompt. Section 2 says the divergence does not appear on that kind of prompt. Fig. 3b also shows OLMo's knowledge effect going negative.

The abstract signals each caveat, but only in wording a fresh reader cannot decode ("measured on the prompt that defines the knowledge labels", "adds only +0.04").

## Strengths

1. The 2x2 design that pulls popularity apart from model-specific knowledge is stated cleanly in one paragraph (l.27-31). Its prediction is clear: abstention driven by uncertainty should follow knowledge, and abstention driven by familiarity should follow popularity.
2. Fig. 1 carries its claim at a glance. The colour coding (popularity orange, knowledge green) stays consistent across Figs. 1c and 2.
3. Reporting is unusually candid: the judge kappa bar and the Llama-only restriction, the failed onset prediction, the never-abstained control that undercuts the paper's own steering result, and the post-hoc labels.
4. Every effect comes with a bootstrap interval and an explicit definition (l.65-67).
5. Related work places the contribution sharply against Ferrando et al. and Kumaran et al. (uninstructed, free-form abstention, with an independent knowledge axis).

## Questions (decision-relevant)

1. What is Llama's first-token answer entropy, or its hedge log-odds, at the last position of the **free** prompt, by cell? If that readout also follows popularity, the dissociation is a property of the prompt rather than of the two readouts, and the title would need to change.
2. What are OLMo's (and Gemma-2-2B's) knowledge effects in the pre-fixed window, with intervals? If OLMo's is negative, the claim in Sec. 3.3 holds for only two of four models.
3. Were Gemma's steered replies in Fig. 2a checked by the two judges? Table 2 gives the regex detector a recall of 0.48 against them for Gemma.

## Recommendation

**Minor revision.** The core abstention result is clear and well supported for Llama, and none of the issues above needs a new experiment. The main problem is that the title, abstract and figure titles say more than the body. Each of M1-M9 can be fixed by rewording or reordering within the current length. If question 1 shows free-prompt entropy following popularity, I would move to major revision.
