### Decision: ACCEPT

Dear authors,

This revision ran six of the eight analyses that round 2 encouraged, and the Llama result survives all of them. It holds under independent knowledge labels: the 27 obscure facts still known abstain at 0.46, and the difference is +0.32 [+0.16, +0.49]. It holds with a third judge (+0.44) and with an entity-knowledge covariate, which shrinks the effect by only 5%. In an item-level model, popularity (+0.29) survives forced-answer entropy. An independent audit found every printed number correct. The known-arm observation is 0.43 against 0.00 abstention at forced-answer entropies of about 0.95 and 0.86 nats. That is a correct, clearly scoped and interesting result of the kind the UniReps call asks for, so the paper is accepted.

The problems are again in the secondary claims, and several are new this round:
- the abstract and §3.2 treat Gemma's near-floor null as the absence of a preference, and never say that your pre-registered four-family test returned REPLICATES for Gemma;
- §3.3 says the instruct preference is "not seen in base models", but your own full-readout panel shows it in three of four;
- "only through its abstention component" depends on an abstention direction fitted mostly on obscure items.

These must be corrected at camera-ready (requirements 1-3). The paper must also stop presenting itself as a test of the misfire idea, because neither model-side measure shows that the obscure names are unfamiliar to Llama, and referential ambiguity is still open (requirement 5).

The methods referee raises three points that limit how the 0.43 and the +0.30 can be read, so they must be disclosed (requirement 4). First, the headline contrast equals obscure-known minus popular-unknown abstention. Second, the popular-unknown cell has not been checked for alias misses. Third, about a quarter of the "I don't know" replies in the deciding cell may contain the correct answer.

Please add no claims at camera-ready beyond the changes listed below. Each revision has removed the previous round's overstatements and added new ones in the new analyses.

### Referee recommendations as given

No referee recommends rejection. The red-team memo makes no formal recommendation, but it argues for rejection; its view is listed first.

- **Red team (attack memo):** no formal recommendation. It concludes: "what remains is a page-view correlate of one model's hedging with no identified cause". Its main grounds are that page views are not shown to be familiarity to the model, the decisive cell is thin, and referential ambiguity is not excluded.
- **Adjudication of the memo:** "No point is critical". P1 (contribution framing) and P3 (ambiguity) are major, and P3 is unresolved. The paper "survives as a 4-page workshop extended abstract only if it changes what it says it contributes."
- **Methods: minor revision.** "The core result is supported at the standard of a 4-page UniReps extended abstract." The referee would move to major revision if the popular-unknown audit or the consensus-label analysis gave an interval that includes zero.
- **Framing: minor revision.** "If those claims are re-scoped, I would support acceptance." The claims meant are the cross-family abstention difference, the base-versus-instruct origin, "main driver", and the link to UniReps.
- **Clarity: minor revision.** All the points can be fixed by rewording, and the space can be found by cutting §4 and the onset sentence.
- **Numbers audit:** no recommendation; it does not judge the science. "No numeric value is wrong." It finds 24 wording mismatches from 20 distinct issues: 2 critical (C1, C2), 3 moderate (M-a, M-b, M-c) and 15 minor.

The venue allows only ACCEPT or REJECT. I did not average these recommendations. The decision rests on the evidence (Chair note N11).
