The paper presents itself as a factorial test of the familiarity-misfire hypothesis (lines 25-34, 86-87), but it never shows that its manipulated variable is familiarity to the model, and its own evidence says it is not. Popularity is the page-view decile of one Wikipedia article (lines 39-41). The only model-side familiarity measure, name surprisal, correlates with page views at Spearman -0.001 and does not predict abstention (Appendix F, lines 482-486). Entity knowledge is higher for the decisive obscure-known items than for popular ones (0.83 vs 0.75, lines 80, 414). So in the cell that carries the paper (n = 87, falling to n = 27 under independent labels, line 76), Llama knows the fact and knows the entity, and nothing shows the name is unfamiliar to it, yet it says "I don't know". The obvious unexcluded cause is referential ambiguity. Low-view PopQA subjects are often obscure bearers of common names, which the authors concede ("New York" as a book title, line 487). The free prompt poses the bare question (line 52), whereas "The answer is" forces a commitment. No homonymy control is run, and a judge label for "asks which entity is meant" (line 55), never checked by humans (line 57), is no substitute. Equating surface form alone removes 43% of the matched-pair gap (lines 431-432). Since steering and the lens isolate nothing (line 133), what remains is a page-view correlate of one model's hedging with no identified cause.
