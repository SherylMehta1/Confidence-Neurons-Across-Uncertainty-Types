    Saying "I Don't Know" Tracks Familiarity,
      Answer Uncertainty Tracks Knowledge

    Abstract

1   When a language model answers a factual question with "I don't know",

2   is it reporting uncertainty about the answer? Crossing entity popularity

3   in PopQA with knowledge labels from the model's own sampled answers

4   separates familiarity with a question's subject from knowledge of the fact.

5   The abstentions of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct follow popularity (effect +0.39) far

6   more than knowledge (+0.09), while its first-token answer entropy follows

7   knowledge, and the entropy pattern holds in four model families. Steering

8   along an obscure-minus-popular direction makes Llama and Gemma-2 say "I

9   don't know" about facts they answer correctly, mostly through components

10  shared with abstention itself. A Jacobian lens finds the familiarity signal

11  at mid-depth in base models of three families. Abstention and answer

12  uncertainty are different variables, and the abstention variable resembles

13  the cue-familiarity heuristic behind human feelings of knowing.

1 14 Introduction

15 A model that says "I don't know" when it lacks a fact avoids the most damaging kind of
16 hallucination [Kalai et al., 2025]. Methods that predict when a model will fail mostly read its
17 uncertainty over answers, through self-evaluation [Kadavath et al., 2022], semantic entropy
18 [Kuhn et al., 2023] or entropy-regulating neurons [Stolfo et al., 2024]. The implicit account
19 is that abstention is uncertainty made audible.

20 Two lines of evidence suggest a second variable. People's sense that they can answer a
21 question follows how familiar its terms are, not whether the answer is stored [Reder and
22 Ritter, 1992, Schwartz and Metcalfe, 1992]. In language models, "known entity" features
23 suppress a default "can't answer" response [Ferrando et al., 2025, Lindsey et al., 2025], but
24 these studies call an entity known when the model answers questions about it correctly, so
25 familiarity and knowledge move together.

26 We separate the two. From PopQA [Mallen et al., 2023] we take questions about obscure
27 and popular entities and label each one known or unknown from the model's own answers
28 [Gekhman et al., 2024], so that familiarity and knowledge vary independently. Abstention
29 that reports answer uncertainty should follow knowledge, as answer entropy does; a familiarity
30 heuristic should follow popularity, even for facts the model always answers correctly.

31 We find three things. (i) The abstentions of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct follow popularity (+0.39)
32 more than knowledge (+0.09), and we show that answer uncertainty follows knowledge in
33 all four families tested (Section 3.1). (ii) An obscure-minus-popular direction added to the
34 residual stream makes Llama and Gemma-2-9B say "I don't know" about popular facts they
35 know, largely through components shared with abstention (Section 3.2). (iii) The familiarity

2 38 Setup

39 Questions and knowledge labels. We use PopQA questions from 16 relations. Within
40 each relation, subjects in the bottom and top deciles of Wikipedia page views form the
41 obscure and popular arms. For each model, a greedy answer and ten samples (T = 0.7) under
42 the forced-answer prompt below are graded against the gold aliases. A fact is known if the
43 greedy answer and at least 90% of samples are correct and unknown if none is; the rest are
44 dropped. This leaves 2,303 questions for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct [Grattafiori et al., 2024] and
45 2,392, 2,282 and 2,378 for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct [Qwen et al., 2024], Gemma-2-9B-it [Team
46 et al., 2024] and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 [Jiang et al., 2023].

47 Two prompts, by necessity. Answer uncertainty is measured with a forced-answer
48 prompt: the chat template plus the assistant prefix "The answer is". The prefix makes the
49 next token the start of an answer, so its entropy is a spread over answers rather than over
50 preambles such as "Sure". The prefix also removes the option to decline, so abstention is
51 measured with a free prompt, the chat template alone. The prompt change could contribute
52 to the divergence. Against this, the log-odds of hedge tokens against answer tokens at the
53 forced-answer position still carry the popularity signal (Gemma: popularity +1.79, knowledge
54 -0.32; in Llama both effects are large), and the steering and lens analyses each use one
55 prompt.

56 Measuring "I don't know". For up to 100 questions per cell (387 in total) we draw
57 three free replies (T = 0.7, up to 96 tokens). Two LLM judges (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and
58 Gemma-2-27B-it) label each reply DONT_KNOW, CLARIFY or ANSWER, and a reply
59 abstains when both say DONT_KNOW. Judge agreement on Llama is  = 0.62, just above
60 the bar of 0.60 fixed before the run. For Qwen, Gemma and Mistral it falls below ( = 0.30,
61 0.47, 0.39) and these models rarely abstain (Appendix B), so abstention is reported for
62 Llama only. The judges were not validated against human labels.

3 67 Results

68 3.1 Saying "I don't know" follows popularity; answer uncertainty follows

69  knowledge

70 Llama's abstentions follow popularity (Figure 1a). For obscure entities it says "I don't know"
71 in 0.48 of replies when it lacks the fact and in 0.43 when it knows it; for popular entities, in
72 0.13 and 0.00. The popularity effect (+0.39 [+0.33, +0.45]) exceeds the knowledge effect
73 (+0.09 [+0.03, +0.15]) by +0.30 [+0.20, +0.40]. Knowledge still matters, at about a quarter
74 of the popularity effect. The difference keeps its sign under three other measurements,
75 including a regex detector with recall 0.99 on these replies (Appendix A).

76 Answer uncertainty shows the reverse (Figure 1b): entropy rises by +2.14 nats when Llama
77 lacks the fact and by +0.51 when the entity is obscure. In every family the knowledge effect
78 is at least four times the popularity effect (Figure 1c).

79 A post hoc check rules out name spelling as the main driver. In a regression over the full
80 Llama pool with relation fixed effects, popularity predicts abstention (regex measure) at
81 -1.87 logits per standard deviation, and at -1.79 after controlling for the name's word
82 frequency, token count and token rarity (Appendix A). The two readouts thus track different
83 variables, and abstention matches the cue-familiarity account of human feelings of knowing
84 [Reder and Ritter, 1992, Schwartz and Metcalfe, 1992].

85 3.2 Steering along the obscure-minus-popular direction produces "I don't

86  know" about known facts

87 On a random half of each model's questions we fitted two directions at the last position of the
88 free prompt: obscure-minus-popular (difference of cell means, averaged over knowledge) and
89 unknown-minus-known [Arditi et al., 2024, Rimsky et al., 2024]. On 150 held-out popular
90 questions the model knows, we added a direction to the residual stream at layer 17 of 32
91 (Llama) or 23 of 42 (Gemma-2-9B-it), at the last prompt position and every generated
92 one. All directions, including five random ones, had the norm of a dose chosen on the
93 obscure-minus-popular direction alone (1× and 4× its natural norm).

94 The obscure-minus-popular direction raised Llama's abstention rate from 0.02 to 0.29, against
95 0.09 for unknown-minus-known and 0.03 for random directions. In Gemma it rose from 0.00
96 to 0.22 while no control moved it (Figure 2a). There, the unknown-minus-known direction
97 instead raised answer entropy more (difference +0.68 [+0.59, +0.77]), a double dissociation;
98 in Llama both raised entropy similarly (-0.03 [-0.11, +0.05]; Figure 2b).

99 This shows control, not that the model uses familiarity as such. A version fitted only on
100 questions Llama never abstained on raised abstention by just +0.04 [+0.01, +0.07] over
101 random directions, so most of the effect runs through components shared with abstaining.
102 Steering can also push a feature outside its natural range [Che and Li, 2026].

103 3.3 The familiarity signal predates instruction tuning

104 We decoded every layer of four base models with the Jacobian lens and its official prefitted
105 lenses [Gurnee et al., 2026], reading the log-odds of a fixed set of hedge tokens against the
106 answer token at the end of "Question: . . . Answer: The answer is". In all four models
107 the obscure-minus-popular effect is positive in the pre-set mid-depth window (Figure 3a):
108 from +0.29 [+0.19, +0.38] in OLMo-3-7B [Olmo et al., 2025] to +0.67 [+0.59, +0.75] in
109 Llama-3.1-8B, with Gemma-2-9B and 2B at +0.39 and +0.40; all intervals exclude zero.

110 One prediction failed. Human feelings of knowing are thought to draw on familiarity early
111 and on retrieval later [Koriat and Levy-Sadot, 2001], so we predicted that the familiarity
112 effect would appear at a shallower depth than the knowledge effect. Across eight base
113 and instruct models our criterion returned 1 supporting, 1 contradicting and 6 undecided
114 outcomes; the two signals emerge together, at relative depth 0.45­0.74 (Figure 3b).

4 115 Related work

116 Entity-recognition features gate refusal in Gemma-2 and Claude 3.5 Haiku [Ferrando et al.,
117 2025, Lindsey et al., 2025]. Brzezinka [2026] distinguish graded familiarity probes from the
118 policy that turns them into abstention, without a knowledge axis. Nguyen et al. [2026] trace
119 a commit-or-abstain circuit across ten models. Fine-tuning on unfamiliar examples shapes
120 responses to unfamiliar queries [Kang et al., 2025], a possible origin of the heuristic. Most
121 uncertainty readouts use the output distribution [Kadavath et al., 2022, Kuhn et al., 2023,
122 Stolfo et al., 2024, Gurnee et al., 2024]; Ji et al. [2025] find verbal uncertainty to be a linear
123 feature only moderately correlated with semantic uncertainty, and we add what each variable
124 follows. Representations converge across models [Huh et al., 2024]; a strict uncertainty
125 representation should not depend on its source [Lippl et al., 2024], yet the abstention variable
126 depends on one, familiarity.

5 127 Limitations and conclusion

128 Abstention is measured reliably in one model; the other three families rarely say "I don't
129 know" and the judges disagreed on them. The judges were not validated against human
130 labels, and their agreement on Llama only just clears its bar, although four measurements
131 agree in sign. Answer uncertainty is first-token entropy under a forced-answer prompt, on
132 single-entity PopQA questions and 7­9B models.

133 Answer uncertainty follows knowledge while abstention follows familiarity, so calibration
134 work should measure both. If familiarity reflects training-text exposure, which model families
135 largely share, then two models' abstentions should agree on obscure names even where only
136 one of them knows the fact.

C 420 Timing, neurons and the circuit

421 In Llama, the lens-decoded abstention readout on matched pairs of obscure and popular
422 questions reaches half its final size at layer 16, while the entropy gap does so at layer 28, 12
423 layers later. Neurons selected for their correlation with output entropy in Llama and Qwen
424 behaved as word-frequency and temperature neurons rather than as uncertainty detectors.
425 A set of 20 attention heads and 100 MLP neurons found by activation patching [Wang et al.,
426 2023, Syed et al., 2024] reproduces 0.83 [0.78, 0.87] of the first-token abstention log-odds
427 difference between matched pairs. In generation, removing its state removes abstention
428 in 0.98 of cases, but injecting it produces abstention in only 0.40, which fell short of the
429 behavioural bar we had fixed in advance; we therefore do not use the circuit as evidence in
430 the main text. Shared uncertainty circuits [Teplica et al., 2025] and a similar asymmetry
431 between releasing and restoring refusal [Liu et al., 2026] have been reported before.
