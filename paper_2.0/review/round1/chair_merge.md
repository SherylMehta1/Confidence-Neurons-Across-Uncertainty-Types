# Area chair merge: round 1

**Manuscript:** "Saying 'I Don't Know' Tracks Familiarity, Answer Uncertainty Tracks Knowledge" (UniReps 2026, extended-abstract track, 4-page main text, non-archival)
**Referee reports merged:** methods and statistics (M), framing and novelty (Fr), clarity and the fresh reader (Cl). Referee finding ids are given as M-F#, Fr-F#, Cl-F#.

**Recommendations as given:** Methods: **major revision**. Framing: **major revision**. Clarity: **minor revision**, and the clarity referee will move to major revision if the Llama numbers for the prompt-confound check show a knowledge effect larger than the popularity effect. No referee recommends rejection.

**Literature claims:** "Referee-verified" means the referee says they opened the source. The chair has not re-checked any literature claim. Claims that no referee verified are marked [unverified].

---

## Overall assessment

No referee recommends rejection: methods and framing recommend major revision, and clarity recommends minor revision, conditional on the Llama prompt-confound numbers. All three referees value the crossing of entity popularity with knowledge labels taken from the model's own samples, and the Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct result it yields (0.43 abstention on obscure facts the model knows, against 0.00 on popular ones), and all three credit the paper for reporting its failed predictions. All three also find that the title, abstract and conclusion claim more than the evidence supports: abstention is measured in one model, the steering effect runs mostly through components shared with abstention, and the base-model lens readout moves with knowledge as much as with popularity. Beyond presentation, the methods and framing referees raise design threats to the headline cell that the manuscript cannot yet rule out: obscure "known" facts may be guessable, the knowledge labels come from the same sampling distribution that the entropy readout measures, and alias grading makes the labels noisy. The framing referee adds that the familiarity-without-knowledge hypothesis, steering on known entities and the base-model origin all have uncredited prior work, and that the closest contrary evidence is not cited. Most fixes are rewording within the page limit, plus analyses on existing outputs and one or two cheap new experiments (independent knowledge labels, a guessing baseline), and those experiments carry the most weight for the headline.

## Disagreements

**D1. Recommendation level (methods and framing: major revision; clarity: minor revision).**
- *Methods and framing:* the headline cell faces design threats that text edits cannot remove: guessability (I6), circular labels (I10), noisy labels (I11). Framing adds uncredited prior work (I13-I15).
- *Clarity:* the core result is internally consistent, and the problems are gaps between what a skim conveys and what the body shows. These are fixable within the page limit, except the Llama numbers in I2 and the dose criterion in I9. Clarity will move to major revision if Llama's forced-prompt knowledge effect exceeds its popularity effect.
- *Deciding passages:* App. A l.417-418 (the manuscript itself concedes that some obscure names contain their answer); §2 l.41-44 (labels and entropy share the forced-prompt sampling distribution); l.53-54 (Llama values not given).
- *What the evidence favours:* the clarity lens did not assess the design threats, so on those this is not a direct contradiction. The manuscript reports neither relation composition nor guessability-filtered results, so it cannot rule out I6. On that point the evidence favours the view that the headline needs more than rewording. Whether it needs more than rewording *and* analyses on existing outputs stays unresolved until the authors report I2 and I6. Each recommendation is reported as given and not averaged.

**D2. What the never-abstained control shows (Fr and Cl strengths vs M-F7).**
- *Framing* (strength 3) calls it "the most informative result in §3.2" and proposes that the intro say "the familiarity-specific component contributes only +0.04" (Fr-F3). *Clarity* (strength 4, Cl-F2) praises it and proposes abstract wording built on the +0.04.
- *Methods* (M-F7) says the control conditions on the outcome. Under the familiarity hypothesis, never-abstained obscure items are those whose familiarity signal is high, so a small effect is predicted by both accounts and the +0.04 cannot be read as the size of a familiarity-specific component.
- *Deciding passage:* §3.2 l.99-101 together with l.87-88 (directions fitted at the free-prompt position, where abstention is upcoming). The fit's sample size is not reported (I38).
- *What the evidence favours:* methods. The manuscript gives no evidence that the familiarity contrast survives in the restricted fit, so +0.04 should not be quoted as a bound on a familiarity-specific contribution until a control that does not condition on abstention (I16/I17) is run. All three referees agree that l.99's cautious wording ("This shows control, not that the model uses familiarity as such") is appropriate. Fr-F3's and Cl-F2's suggested sentences should therefore drop the inference from +0.04 to a component size.

**D3. How to fix the Lippl et al. sentence (I33).**
- *Clarity:* keep the argument and define the terms: a representation of uncertainty "as such" should not depend on what caused the uncertainty, yet abstention depends on familiarity.
- *Framing:* the argument misapplies Lippl et al.'s source-invariance definition (referee-verified). Abstention responding to familiarity when there is no answer uncertainty fits "representational indeterminacy": the variable is a correlate of uncertainty, not a representation of it.
- *Deciding passage:* l.124-126, read against l.70-72 and Fig. 1b. The obscure-known cell abstains at 0.43 while its entropy is low (about 0.95 nats, read off Fig. 1b by Fr).
- *What the evidence favours:* framing. In that cell familiarity does not act as a source of answer uncertainty, so the "depends on its source" framing does not fit the paper's own data.

**D4. Where OLMo-3-7B's familiarity curve goes negative (I5).**
- *Framing:* it "dips below zero inside the window and is clearly negative after about 0.7".
- *Methods and clarity:* it is negative late in the network, after about 0.75.
- *Deciding passage:* Fig. 3a and l.107-109 (OLMo window mean +0.29 [+0.19, +0.38]).
- *What the evidence favours:* at print resolution, the chair reads the pronounced negative excursion as lying to the right of the shaded window, which favours methods and clarity on its location. Whether the curve crosses zero inside the window cannot be read from the figure, so that point is unresolved. The authors should report per-layer values. All three agree the reversal must be discussed.

**D5. Severity of the prompt-confound gap (I2): framing minor vs methods and clarity major.**
- *Framing* (Fr-F16) treats the forced-prompt hedge readout as the only evidence that the abstention pattern extends beyond Llama.
- *Methods and clarity* treat it as the only defence of the Llama dissociation itself against the prompt change.
- *Deciding passage:* l.51-52, "The prompt change could contribute to the divergence", where the paper concedes the threat.
- *What the evidence favours:* major. The missing Llama values are exactly the case the confound threatens.

**D6. Severity of "rules out name spelling" (I3): clarity major vs methods and framing minor.**
- *Deciding passage:* App. A l.415-416, where the matched-pair gap falls from +0.76 to +0.43 at equal surface form.
- *What the evidence favours:* the main text's claim contradicts the paper's own appendix on a point that bears directly on the familiarity reading of the headline, so the chair keeps major. The fix is rewording plus regression details.

**D7. "At least four times" (I41): clarity "consistent" vs methods "rests on point estimates".**
- These are compatible. Both compute about 4.2 for Llama from point estimates.
- Methods' concern that the interval's lower bound is "likely" below 4 is untested. It stays unresolved until the authors report the interval.

**D8. Gemma-2-9B knowledge-effect magnitude in Fig. 3b (I4).**
- *Methods:* it "peaks around +1.7 against about +1.0 for familiarity". *Framing:* it rises "to about +1" in the window.
- Both conclude that the knowledge effect is comparable to or larger than the familiarity effect. The peak lies just outside the shaded window. This is not a substantive split, and the window values requested in I4 settle it.

**D9. Space-saving cut vs a methods concern (I12 vs I18).**
- *Clarity* (Cl-F3) proposes cutting l.102, the Che and Li out-of-range caveat, to pay for the popularity-as-proxy clause.
- *Methods* (M-F11) builds a major issue on that caveat being unchecked.
- *What the evidence favours:* unresolved as a matter of evidence. Practically, if l.102 is cut, the caveat and the I18 checks should appear in the appendix with a pointer from §3.2.

## Chair notes (the chair's own observations; no referee raised these)

These are separate from the referees' assessments and are not weighed in the recommendations above.

- **C1. Template.** The page footer reads "Submitted to 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)". The authors should confirm they are using the UniReps workshop style file and footer.
- **C2. Base and instruct model sets differ.** The lens study uses Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-2-2B and OLMo-3-7B base models. The behavioural study uses Llama, Qwen2.5, Gemma-2-9B and Mistral instruct models. Only Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2-9B appear on both sides. "Predates instruction tuning" (l.36-37) is therefore partly a comparison across different models. This bears on I4.
- **C3. Same-family judges.** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct judges Qwen2.5-7B replies, and Gemma-2-27B-it judges Gemma-2-9B replies (l.57-58). Same-family labelling bias may contribute to the judge disagreement in those families. A sentence or a cross-family check would help.
- **C4. Dropped intermediate items.** The labelling rule drops facts that are neither reliably known nor never known (l.43-44), but the number dropped per model and arm is not reported. The dropped items are partially known facts, where an uncertainty-based account and a familiarity account would differ most. Reporting the counts, and abstention on these items in the appendix, would be informative. This is related to I43 but distinct from it.
- **C5. Window bounds.** The pre-set mid-depth window is never stated numerically. In Fig. 3 the shading appears to span roughly 0.33-0.66, while l.114 reports emergence at 0.45-0.74. Stating the bounds would make I22 and I28 easier to resolve.
- **C6. Citations no referee checked [unverified].** No referee reports checking: Liu et al. 2026 (arXiv 2608.15772); Teplica et al. 2025; Kang et al. 2025; Kalai et al. 2025; the human feeling-of-knowing papers that carry the paper's analogy (Reder and Ritter 1992; Schwartz and Metcalfe 1992; Koriat and Levy-Sadot 2001). Their content claims are [unverified]. The chair verified no literature claim.
- **C7. Bibliography detail.** The Ferrando et al. entry gives only an OpenReview URL with no venue. The framing referee identifies it as ICLR 2025 (referee-verified).
- **C8. Availability.** The manuscript has no code or data availability statement. This is optional for a non-archival track, but it would support the pre-registration claims (I27).
