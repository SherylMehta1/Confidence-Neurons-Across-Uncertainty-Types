    In Llama, Saying "I Don't Know" Tracks
    Entity Popularity More Than Knowledge

    Abstract

1   We investigate what a language model's "I don't know" tracks: whether the

2   model lacks the fact, or how familiar the entity is. We cross entity popularity

3   (Wikipedia page views in PopQA) with knowledge labels taken from the

4   model's own sampled answers. We find that in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, saying

5   "I don't know" tracks popularity more than knowledge. On obscure entities,

6   Llama says "I don't know" to 43% of questions it answers correctly in at

7   least 9 of 10 forced samples; on popular entities the rate is 0%, although its

8   answer entropy is similar in the two cases. The gap holds with knowledge

9   relabeled from a second prompt, with unknown facts regraded by a larger

10  model, and when only replies that name no answer count. Gemma-2-9B

11  rarely abstains under a plain prompt. When the prompt permits "I don't

12  know", however, its abstention also tracks popularity more than knowledge.

13  As a control, answer entropy follows knowledge, as the labeling implies.

14  Steering and logit-lens readouts do not isolate a familiarity mechanism, so

15  our evidence shows what abstention tracks, not why.

1 16 Introduction

17 Saying "I don't know" when a fact is missing avoids the worst hallucinations [Kalai et al.,
18 2025]. Confidence estimates usually read uncertainty over answers [Kadavath et al., 2022,
19 Kuhn et al., 2023, Stolfo et al., 2024], and models use such confidence when a prompt offers
20 abstention [Kumaran et al., 2026]. However, verbal and answer-level uncertainty diverge [Ji
21 et al., 2025], so what a model's "I don't know" actually tracks is less clear.

22 One candidate is familiarity. In people, the sense of being able to answer depends on how
23 familiar a question's terms are as well as on whether the answer can be retrieved [Reder
24 and Ritter, 1992, Schwartz and Metcalfe, 1992, Koriat and Levy-Sadot, 2001]. In language
25 models, "known entity" features suppress a default "can't answer" response. Lindsey et al.
26 [2025] propose that a familiar name can trigger these features when the fact is missing, so
27 the model answers when it should abstain. We call this a misfire. The converse failure is an
28 unfamiliar name that does not suppress the default: the model then abstains on a fact it
29 knows.

30 Testing this requires separating familiarity from knowledge. Existing tests define an entity
31 as known when the model answers about it correctly [Ferrando et al., 2025], so the two
32 move together. Familiarity has been separated from factual reliability in representations
33 [Brzezinka, 2026a] and shown to shape hallucination through fine-tuning data [Kang et al.,
34 2025]. It has not been crossed with knowledge in abstention behavior; this crossing is what
35 is new here.

40 In Llama, they do (Figure 1, Table 1). This also bears on representational convergence [Huh
41 et al., 2024]: do models that agree on answer uncertainty also agree on what makes them
42 abstain? Only Llama and, under a permissive prompt, Gemma let us ask.

43 Main Contributions.

44 1. Popularity Over Knowledge in Llama: Llama's popularity preference is +0.30 [+0.20,
45 +0.40] (Section 3.1). It survives four label and measurement checks.
46 2. Same Preference in Gemma When Permitted: under a permissive prompt, Gemma's
47 preference is +0.30 [+0.19, +0.40] (Section 3.2). Under the plain prompt it rarely abstains.
48 3. No Isolated Familiarity Mechanism: steering moves a generic abstention signal, and
49 a base-model lens responds to both factors (Section 3.3).

50 Together, these findings mean that, in this extreme-contrast design, Llama's answer uncer-
51 tainty alone predicts its abstention poorly.

2 52 Setup

53 Questions & Labels. We use the 16 relations of PopQA. Within each relation, subjects in
54 the bottom and top deciles of Wikipedia page views (a proxy for familiarity) form the obscure
55 and popular arms. For each model, we grade a greedy answer and ten samples (T = 0.7)
56 against gold aliases, under a forced-answer prompt (the chat template plus the assistant
57 prefix "The answer is"). A fact is known if the greedy answer and at least nine samples are
58 correct, and unknown if no sample is; the band between is dropped. This leaves 2,303, 2,392,
59 2,282 and 2,378 questions for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it
60 and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 [Grattafiori et al., 2024, Qwen et al., 2024, Team et al., 2024,
61 Jiang et al., 2023] (cell counts in Appendix A).

62 Two Readouts. Answer uncertainty is the first-token entropy under the forced-answer
63 prompt. The knowledge labels come from the same distribution, so entropy should follow
64 knowledge; it serves as a control, and we also relabel knowledge from an independent prompt.
65 Abstention is measured with a free prompt (the chat template alone): for up to 100 questions
66 per popularity × knowledge cell (387 in total), we draw three replies (T = 0.7, up to
67 96 tokens). Two LLM judges (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, Gemma-2-27B-it) label each reply
68 DONT_KNOW, CLARIFY (asks which entity is meant) or ANSWER. A reply abstains
69 when both say DONT_KNOW. On Llama the judges agree at  = 0.62 [0.57, 0.67], above a
70 pre-specified bar of 0.60; on the other families they do not (Section 3.2).

71 Effects. The popularity effect is the obscure-minus-popular difference in a readout, averaged
72 over the two knowledge levels. The knowledge effect is the unknown-minus-known difference,
73 averaged over popularity. Their difference (popularity effect minus knowledge effect) is
74 the model's popularity preference: positive when abstention follows popularity more than
75 knowledge. In this balanced 2 × 2, it equals abstention on obscure known facts minus
76 abstention on popular unknown facts. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps over questions
77 within cells, not clustered by relation. Unless marked post hoc, every analysis followed a
78 plan written and hashed before it ran (pre-specified; Appendix G).

89 Label & Measurement Checks: The Preference Survives. Relabeling knowledge
90 with a paraphrased question and prefix agrees with the original labels at  = 0.79. Of the 87
91 obscure known facts, 27 stay known; these abstain at 0.46. The preference is +0.32 [+0.16,
92 +0.49], and entropy still follows knowledge. Popular entities may have valid answers outside
93 PopQA's aliases. We therefore had Llama-3.3-70B regrade forced answers by meaning. It
94 found 9 of the 100 popular unknown facts known and 31 in between; with its labels, the
95 preference is +0.23 [+0.12, +0.35]. The "I don't know" replies are refusals, not hedged
96 answers. With Llama-3.3-70B as a third judge and a majority rule,  = 0.74 and the
97 preference is +0.44. At most 0.03 of the "I don't know" replies in any cell contain the gold
98 answer (post hoc). Counting only replies that the 70B judge labels as declining without
99 naming any candidate gives +0.43 [+0.31, +0.54].

100 Recognition & Ambiguity: Neither Explains It. Llama describes the entity correctly
101 in 0.83 of its completions for obscure known facts and 0.75 for popular known ones. Adding
102 this LLM-graded recognition score to a regression shrinks the popularity coefficient by 5%.
103 If obscure names were ambiguous, Llama might hedge for that reason. The data point the
104 other way (post hoc): popular names are shared with more Wikidata entities (median 11­13,
105 against 1 for obscure known facts), and obscure known facts with a unique name still draw
106 "I don't know" in 0.43 of replies. Name spelling explains part of the gap between matched
107 pairs of known facts, but barely changes the popularity coefficient over all questions (post
108 hoc; Appendix A).

109 Familiarity: Not Shown Directly. Neither model-side measure shows the obscure names
110 to be unfamiliar to Llama: name surprisal is uncorrelated with page views (Appendix F),

113 Within One Prompt: The Split Remains. At the forced-answer position, the first
114 token's lean toward hedge tokens (refusal openers such as " I", " not", " unknown") follows
115 popularity and knowledge equally (-0.12 [-0.72, +0.45]; Appendix B). After seeing this,
116 we specified a reading at the free prompt, where the log-odds of a first token "I" follow
117 popularity (+8.4 vs. +3.1), while its entropy follows knowledge (+0.16 vs. +0.05 nats).

118 3.2 Other Families: Gemma Tracks Popularity When Permitted

119 Plain Prompt: Too Few Abstentions to Measure. Entropy follows knowledge in all
120 four families (Figure 1c), but Qwen, Gemma and Mistral rarely say "I don't know", and the
121 judges disagree on them. With a third judge, Gemma reaches  = 0.64 but abstains in at most
122 0.11 of replies in any cell, so its preference cannot be estimated. A pre-specified four-family
123 test on the forced-answer hedge readout found both of its conditions in Gemma and one in
124 Qwen and Mistral, but that readout shows no preference in Llama either (Appendix B).

125 Permissive Prompt: Same Preference in Gemma. We then ended the question with
126 "If you do not know the answer, say that you do not know". Both models abstain often, and
127 three judges agree ( = 0.71, 0.67). The popularity preference is +0.47 [+0.35, +0.59] for
128 Llama and +0.30 [+0.19, +0.40] for Gemma. Gemma says "I don't know" to 0.41 of obscure
129 known questions and 0.00 of popular known ones. Cross-model agreement on that readout,
130 beyond shared labels, met a post hoc rule for one of three pairs (Appendix E).

131 3.3 Mechanistic Tests: No Familiarity Cause Isolated

132 Steering: A Generic Abstention Signal. Like work steering refusal along recognition
133 directions [Ferrando et al., 2025, Brzezinka, 2026b], we fitted three directions on half of
134 the questions [Arditi et al., 2024, Rimsky et al., 2024]: obscure-minus-popular, unknown-
135 minus-known and abstain-minus-answer. We added each at a multiple (the dose) of the
136 obscure-minus-popular direction's norm, chose the dose on a second set and scored a third
137 (Appendix C). Obscure-minus-popular raised Llama's abstention from 0.01 to 0.10, +0.09
138 [+0.06, +0.12] over random directions (short of the pre-specified +0.10 bar), and Gemma's
139 from 0.00 to 0.04 at 4× the norm (Figure 2a). In Llama, the direction is nearly parallel
140 to the abstention direction (cosine 0.95), partly by construction, since abstaining questions
141 are mostly obscure. Removing that component leaves +0.01 [-0.00, +0.02] (Gemma +0.02
142 [+0.01, +0.03]). Fitted on obscure questions alone, the abstention direction still has cosine
143 0.91, and removing it leaves +0.01 [+0.00, +0.03] (three judges). Obscure-minus-popular
144 beats unknown-minus-known on abstention (+0.06 [+0.02, +0.09]), as the cells' rates predict.
145 In Gemma, unknown-minus-known raises entropy +0.66 [+0.58, +0.74] nats more (Figure 2b;
146 cf. Che and Li, 2026).

147 Lens: Both Factors in Base Models. With prefitted Jacobian lenses [Gurnee et al.,
148 2026], read at the forced-answer position in a pre-specified mid-depth window, popularity
149 raises the hedge-token term in Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-2-2B and OLMo-3-7B
150 [Olmo et al., 2025]. Knowledge raises it about as much (Appendix D, Figure 3). Instruct
151 partners lean further toward knowledge, so this readout does not show where Llama-Instruct's
152 preference arises.

4 153 Limitations

154 Abstention is judged by LLMs not validated against human labels. The design uses only
155 the extreme page-view deciles, facts answered correctly at least nine times in ten or never,
156 and cells of at most 100 questions; models have 2­9B parameters, and questions name a
157 single entity. Hedging driven by ambiguity that the judges do not label as clarification is not
158 excluded, and whether other families share the preference is open.
