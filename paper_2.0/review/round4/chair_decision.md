### Decision: ACCEPT

Dear authors,

**No referee recommends rejection.** All three referees recommend **major revision**, and each says what would make the paper acceptable. The red-team memo makes no formal recommendation. It argues that the headline's "simplest reading is that Llama correctly declines guesses the labeling rule miscounts as knowledge. Nothing in the paper rules that reading out." The adjudication finds that alternative "still coherent" and says the paper "survives at a workshop only if the headline is narrowed or the P2 control is added".

**Recommendations as given:**
- **Methods (Me): major revision.** "If the jointly corrected, relation-clustered preference stays clearly positive and the guessing baseline is low, I would move to minor revision."
- **Framing (Fr): major revision.** The referee lists the changes needed (a scoped title and abstract, an introduction reframed around popularity, a cross-model comparison or an honest venue statement), "plus permissive-prompt runs on Qwen and Mistral". With those, "I would recommend acceptance as an extended abstract."
- **Clarity (Cl): major revision.** "For a workshop that means accept once the issues below are fixed; no new experiments are needed beyond reconciling one number."
- **Red team (RT):** no formal recommendation. It argues against the headline, as quoted above.
- **Adjudication (Adj):** no formal recommendation. Five points are partially answered and one (P2, name cues) is unresolved and major. It accepts "the narrower result" but not "the title and contribution 1 as written".

**Why ACCEPT.** I based the decision on the evidence in the manuscript, not on the vote count.

1. **The behavioural finding is robust in sign.** Under every measure and label variant in Table 2, Llama abstains more on obscure than on popular entities, and the preference stays positive: +0.13 to +0.44 across abstention measures, and +0.19 to +0.32 across knowledge-label variants. All intervals exclude zero.
2. **The popularity effect does not depend on the contested cell.** Among unknown facts alone, the popularity gap is +0.34 [+0.26, +0.43] (l.286). On my arithmetic (Chair note N3), it stays near +0.24 even under the strictest popular-unknown audit.
3. **Nothing printed has been shown wrong, with one exception.** That exception is the gold-alias figures in the obscure-known cell (I4, I15), which must be reconciled.
4. **The paper is candid.** It reports post hoc decisions, failed bars and negative mechanistic results plainly.

What the evidence does **not** support as written is the comparative "more than knowledge". That comparison is the obscure-known minus popular-unknown contrast (l.75-76), and its obscure-known side rests on 87 forced-answer labels. Those labels could come from cues in the name or the relation (I11-I13, I23), and the paper has no control for this. Two further problems sit with that comparison:
- the abstract hides how much the estimate shrinks under label audits (I1, I6);
- the framing still promises a familiarity account that §3.1 withdraws (I7).

These are problems of claim and disclosure, not of the data. A non-archival extended abstract can meet the bar if its claims are cut back to what the data show.

**Acceptance is therefore conditional on the Must-fix list in the Revision roadmap.** Two conditions are central:
1. **The headline.** Run the name-and-relation guessing baseline (I11), or narrow the title, abstract and Contribution 1. After narrowing, the label-robust popularity gap among unknown facts leads, and "more than knowledge" is stated as conditional on forced-answer labels (I19, I23).
2. **The numbers in the deciding cell.** Reconcile them (I4, I15).

Several overstatements this round are new and sit in run-in headings: "The Preference Survives", "Neither Explains It", "The Split Remains", "A Generic Abstention Signal" and "Same Preference in Gemma". Do not add claims at camera-ready beyond what the roadmap asks for (Chair note N4).

### Overall assessment

The paper crosses entity popularity (PopQA page-view deciles) with model-specific knowledge labels, which, as far as the two referees who checked could verify, is new. In Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct it shows that "I don't know" responds strongly to popularity at both knowledge levels: 0.43 against 0.00 on known facts, and 0.48 against 0.13 on unknown facts (Table 1). All three referees accept this dissociation as real in sign, and they praise the paper's transparent pre-specified and post hoc accounting and its restraint about mechanism. The comparative headline is weaker than presented:
- the two cells that define the preference have the least stable labels (27 of 87 obscure-known facts stay known; 48% of popular-unknown questions yield the gold answer in some free reply);
- name and relation cues are not excluded as the source of "known" labels on obscure entities;
- the gold-alias figures in the deciding cell do not reconcile.

The secondary claims (Gemma's "same preference", the "generic abstention signal", the UniReps convergence question) are stated more strongly than the analyses allow, or are left unanswered. Most fixes are rewording or new analyses of outputs the authors already have. The exceptions are a name-only guessing baseline, permissive-prompt runs on two further families and a human audit, and the first of these can be replaced by narrowing the headline.

### Disagreements

**D1. Does the obscure-known cell measure knowledge? (I11-I13, I23)**
- *Position A* (Me F2, Adj P2 "unresolved", RT): consistent correct answers under a forced prefix may be inferred from the name or the relation, and none of the checks can detect that.
- *Position B* (the manuscript's evidence, which Adj says RT skipped): recognition is 0.83; obscure-known facts with a unique name still abstain at 0.43; with guessable answers removed the preference is +0.30; among unknown facts alone the gap is +0.34.
- *Fr and Cl* do not raise guessing. Their silence is not agreement.
- *Deciding passages:* l.55-58; l.90-91; l.100-102; App. A l.257-258, l.266-267, l.283-294.
- *What the evidence favours:* **unresolved on the page.** Position B's evidence does not separate the readings. Recognition is graded against descriptions that the name may itself reveal, the unique-name subset keeps the name, and the guessability filter catches only verbatim and modal-answer cues. The deciding test (a name-only baseline) has not been run. What the evidence does settle is that the popularity effect among unknown facts does not depend on this cell. Hence I23 is a must-fix: the headline must be narrowed, or the control run.

**D2. How serious is label instability? Me F1 (major) vs Adj P3 (minor, presentation).**
- *Deciding passages:* l.89-92; App. A l.258-265.
- *What the evidence favours:* both, on different points, so I split the issue.
  - Adj is right that the result does not depend on the unstable labels. The stable-label re-estimate is +0.32 [+0.16, +0.49], and attrition is similar on the popular side (35 of 100), so the κ reporting gap is minor (I35).
  - Me is right that losing 69% of obscure-known labels under a paraphrase shows "known" is specific to the prompt. That bears on what the headline's "knowledge" means, so it stays major (I5).

**D3. Is the "Within One Prompt" paragraph major (Cl F2) or minor (Me F11, Fr F16)?**
- *Deciding passages:* l.113-117; Table 3 (hedge log-odds, Llama -0.12 [-0.72, +0.45]).
- *What the evidence favours:* major.
  - The heading "The Split Remains" asserts what the pre-specified test did not find.
  - The replacement reading has no intervals or n.
  - Me F11 itself notes that this is the only analysis that addresses the prompt-mismatch alternative to the headline.
  - The fix is cheap: rewording plus intervals from existing outputs.

**D4. What does the recognition score show? Fr F1 and Cl F1 vs Me F16 and Adj P2.**
- *Fr and Cl:* recognition of 0.83 against 0.75 shows that the obscure names are not unfamiliar to Llama, which undercuts the familiarity framing.
- *Me and Adj:* the score is graded against generic Wikidata descriptions that can be inferred from the name, so a higher score on obscure-known facts may mark guessable descriptions, not familiarity.
- *Deciding passages:* l.100-102; App. A l.276-279.
- *What the evidence favours:* **unresolved.** Only a validation on fabricated names (Me F16) decides it. Either way the consequences agree: the familiarity framing must go (I7), and "Neither Explains It" must be softened (I37).

**D5. Ambiguity: Fr (strength 5, "addressed in the right direction") vs Me F17 (it measures label collisions).**
- *Deciding passages:* l.103-106; App. A l.288-291.
- *What the evidence favours:* Me on scope, Fr on direction.
  - The statistic is a Wikidata label count, not the model's own resolution of the name.
  - The unique-name subset (0.43) does run against crude homonymy.
  - The fix is wording ("Wikidata label collisions do not explain it"), with a model-side measure as optional.

**D6. Is the known-cell entropy similarity informative? Fr F18 and Cl F13 vs Adj P5 and RT.**
- *Fr and Cl:* the informative part of Fig. 1b is that entropy is similar for obscure-known and popular-known facts (0.95 vs 0.86 nats), and the title should say so.
- *Adj and RT:* both known cells are selected for at least 9/10 consistent correct answers, so low and similar entropy follows from the labelling.
- *Deciding passages:* l.57-58; l.62-64; Table 1.
- *What the evidence favours:* mostly Adj. The label rule selects both known cells for consistent answers, so similar first-token entropy is expected; it cannot serve as independent evidence of equal knowledge. It remains a correct description (Chair note N6).
- *Common ground:* both sides want "answer uncertainty follows knowledge" demoted from the Fig. 1 title. The authors may state the similarity, with the caveat that it follows from the label rule (I29, I68).

**D7. Gemma's plain-prompt estimate: Me F8 (a precise near-zero estimate; major) vs Fr F10 and Cl F24 (uninformative because of the floor; minor).**
- *Deciding passages:* Table 3 and l.306-307 (+0.01 [-0.03, +0.06]); App. B l.313-315 (the 0.25 criterion); l.121-122 (rates of 0.11 or below).
- *What the evidence favours:* both, on different points.
  - Fr and Cl are right that, with every cell at 0.11 or below, a near-zero difference on the probability scale is not evidence of no preference.
  - Me is right that "cannot be estimated" is false as written, and that a scale-free contrast is both estimable and needed before "Same Preference" can be read across prompts.
  - Round-3 item 1 prescribed the "cannot be estimated" wording, so the current text is partly the previous chair's doing. The wording is minor (I26). The headline use of the permissive result stays major (I20).

**D8. Which scorer should lead for Gemma steering? Cl F5 vs Me F15.**
- *Cl F5:* the main text should report the three-judge numbers, which the paper calls defensible.
- *Me F15:* the amendment adding the third judge has an unexplained date discrepancy, so the pre-amendment scorer should be reported as primary, with the secondary beside it.
- *Deciding passages:* App. C l.333-337; App. G footnote a; Table 3 (Gemma two-judge κ 0.47, below the 0.60 bar).
- *What the evidence favours:*
  - On defensibility, Cl. The paper's own bar disqualifies the two-judge scorer for Gemma.
  - On disclosure, Me. Report both scorers in §3.3 and explain the date discrepancy (I22, I27). Neither number should be shown alone.

**D9. What the steering result contributes: Fr F6 and Fr strength 4 vs Me F10.**
- *Fr:* the abstention-overlap projection is a stronger control than prior work used, and it qualifies published steering claims.
- *Me:* the design cannot separate a generic signal from a familiarity signal, so the residual null says little.
- *Deciding passages:* l.139-143; App. C l.337-348.
- *What the evidence favours:* both claims hold, and they are compatible.
  - As a control, the result shows that the effect over random directions is carried by the overlap with this abstention direction. That is the advance Fr describes (I21).
  - It does not show the signal is "generic" (I10), because the abstention direction is fitted on the same entity questions.

**D10. What would change the recommendation.**
- *Me:* new analyses (joint correction, clustering) plus a guessing baseline.
- *Fr:* reframing, plus permissive runs on two further families.
- *Cl:* rewording plus one reconciliation.
- *How the roadmap uses them:*
  - Me's analyses on existing outputs are Must fix.
  - The guessing baseline is Must fix, with headline narrowing as the accepted alternative (I11, I23).
  - Fr's permissive runs are Should fix (I3), because they bear on Gemma and generality, not on the Llama headline.
