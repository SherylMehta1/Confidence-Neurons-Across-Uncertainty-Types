\begin{abstract}
When a language model says ``I don't know,'' is that a decision, or a symptom of a flat output
distribution? Existing evidence cannot tell: ``uncertain'' prompts are annotated rather than verified, and
single-component accounts conflate the two. We separate them. On gated twin prompts---minimal pairs the
model itself certifies by sampled correctness, entropy gap, and hedging---correlation-selected
``confidence neurons'' decompose into token-frequency and temperature machinery: the spread's plumbing, not
the decision. The decision travels elsewhere. Activation patching in \lm{} and \qw{} shows the entity is
read in the first eight layers and a verdict routed mid-stack by roughly 20 attention heads and 100 MLP
neurons ($0.02\%$ of neurons) that transfer 78--107\% of the held-out hedge readout (overshooting only on
the reverse direction); size-matched random sets transfer 2--4\%. A rank-1 uncertainty direction suffices to switch the hedge on, but cannot switch it off;
un-hedging requires the component set, which carries the decision with little of the spread. An independent Jacobian lens watches the
verdict become a word: `` unknown'' rises from rank ${\sim}1{,}000$ to rank 10 across layers 13--17, about
twelve layers before the twins' entropy gap appears. In \lm{}, hedging is a computed verdict, not felt
entropy; the circuit, though not the directional asymmetry, replicates in \qw{} on a token-level
readout. Confidence methods that read only the output distribution risk measuring the symptom rather
than the decision.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
When a model hedges, the natural reading---and the one the single-component literature encodes
\citep{stolfo2024confidence,gurnee2024universal,roy2025unwanted,vazhentsev2026heads,ferrando2025iknow,ji2025hedging,anthropic2025biology}---is
that hedging is uncertainty made audible: one neuron, one head, one direction, leaking entropy into words.
But no existing dataset checks that the model is actually uncertain on its ``uncertain'' prompts, and no
intervention has separated moving the \emph{distribution} from moving the \emph{decision to say so}. When
we build prompt pairs the model itself certifies and patch between them, the assumption fails: the decision
and the spread travel in different channels. About twenty attention heads and a hundred MLP
neurons---${\sim}2\%$ of heads and $0.02\%$ of neurons---carry 78--107\% of the hedge decision between twins, while every single neuron that
correlation selects turns out to be spread machinery, and a rank-1 direction moves entropy without moving
the hedge. An independent lens can then watch the verdict become a word: across layers 13--17 the token
`` unknown'' rises from rank ${\sim}1{,}000$ to rank 10 at the readout position of the uncertain twin only.
If the hedge is a verdict rather than a symptom, then confidence estimation read off the output distribution
alone measures the wrong channel---and the verdict itself is small enough to audit directly.

Circuit-level accounts exist for adjacent behaviors---\citet{teplica2025sciurus} show across eight models
that uncertainty shares circuits with answer factuality; \citet{zhao2026wired} localize confidence-mover
components for inflated verbalized scores---but none verify a hedging circuit at head$+$neuron granularity
against size-matched random sets, and none dissociate the decision from the spread. We contribute:
(1)~a gated-twin dataset standard with judge-validated grading and reported keep rates; (2)~on that footing,
the decomposition of what correlation-based neuron selection actually finds; (3)~a twin-patching circuit at
head$+$neuron granularity with random-set faithfulness controls, in two models and two uncertainty types;
(4)~the decision-vs-spread dissociation, stress-tested by a pre-stated prediction and an independent lens;
(5)~an explicit account of which of our own criteria the evidence failed, and how follow-ups decided them.

\section{Methods}
\paragraph{Datasets: uncertainty as a (model, prompt) property.}
A twin pair is one manipulation with one change---obscure vs.\ famous PopQA \citep{mallen2023popqa} subject
in the same relation template (\emph{familiarity}); two disagreeing sources vs.\ one
\citep{xie2024conflictqa} (\emph{contested}); many valid answers vs.\ one (\emph{aleatoric})---with
identical chat template and prefill and question length within 3 tokens. A pair enters an arm only if the
model proves the labels \citep{kadavath2022know,gekhman2024slick}: control answered correctly in
$\geq 9/10$ samples (greedy $+$ 10 at $T{=}0.7$, alias-graded), uncertain $0/10$, first-token entropy gap
$\geq 0.5$ nats, and---for the behavioral arm---hedging-rate gap $\geq 0.3$ over free generations. Keep
rates are reported, never tuned: \lm{} familiarity $195/1151$, contested $234/600$, aleatoric $41/120$;
\qw{} $24$, $178$, $39$; a fourth (situated) arm is dropped at $3/600$ with the reason on record. The
gate thresholds were fixed before any gating ran. The alias grader is audited against a Qwen2.5-32B judge
on a stratified sample (agreement 94.6--100\%, $\kappa$ 0.70--1.00); disagreement is \emph{lower} on
hedged answers (1.5\% vs.\ 6.1\%), so grader error does not mimic the behavioral readout.

\paragraph{Neuron-level causal battery.}
Mean-ablation at the readout position with an explicit reference policy and matched control prompts;
sign-flip permutation per cell; two-sided permutation for the uncertain-vs-control interaction; BH-FDR at
$\alpha{=}0.01$; plus adjustments for baseline entropy, motivated by the fact that a flat distribution
amplifies the \emph{magnitude} of any perturbation ($\rho(H,|\Delta H|){=}0.25$ on our data). A caveat we
state up front: the signed shift is uncorrelated with baseline entropy ($\rho{=}{-}0.001$), and on gated
arms the two conditions' entropy supports are disjoint, so the ANCOVA coefficient there is extrapolation;
on gated arms we therefore rely on a random-neuron null and the temperature-matched design (Results).
That null is descriptive, not FDR-controlled: with 100 (familiarity) / 50 (contested) empirical nulls the
smallest attainable $p$ is $1/101$ ($1/51$), above every BH threshold at $\alpha{=}0.01$ over 27
candidates---zero FDR survivors is guaranteed by construction---so there we report effect sizes and
exceedance counts, not significance.
Dose--response, frozen-RMSNorm decomposition, temperature-matched frequency tests, behavioral clamping,
and verbalized confidence complete the battery.

\paragraph{Circuit-level decomposition.}
All circuit claims rest on activation patching between gated twins---reference-free by construction.
(1)~Position$\times$layer maps of residual patching. (2)~Attribution patching \citep{syed2023attribution}
over every head at every position on the hedge-vs-answer log-odds; the top 30 heads \emph{verified by
real patching}. (3)~The same at MLP-block and single-neuron level; top-$N$ neuron sets verified jointly
against size-matched random sets. (4)~Faithfulness \citep{wang2023ioi}: the final head$+$neuron set
patched jointly on held-out pairs, both directions, against random sets, with the criterion
\emph{recovery $>0.7$ with $<2\%$ of components}---fixed in the analysis code before the held-out runs,
but not independently registered, and treated accordingly (\S\ref{sec:undecided}).
(5)~The identical pipeline on a second arm and model, with component-set overlap. Recovery
$=(\text{patched}-\text{target})/(\text{source}-\text{target})$.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{What correlation selects is the spread's machinery.}
Correlation-selected candidates in \lm{} are 9/55 token-frequency neurons by weights and causally so under
a temperature-matched baseline (stable across prompt sets, $r{=}0.52$--$0.62$; $6\times$ random), plus one
general temperature neuron (L31\_N2477: monotonic dose--response $\rho{=}0.86$, $\sim$100\%
RMSNorm-mediated, not uncertainty-specific)---consistent with \citet{du2025posttraining}. On the original
stimuli (overlapping entropy supports; three adjustment schemes agree) and on the gated familiarity arm, no
neuron passes the specificity test. Under an \emph{identified} null---every statistic recomputed for 100
(familiarity) / 50 (contested) random neurons ablated on the same prompts, which inherit the entropy
sensitivity by construction---four familiarity candidates exceed every random control (largest L29\_N5866,
$z{=}{-}6.2$; ${\sim}0.3$ of the 27 would beat all nulls by chance) but at $\leq 0.013$ nats, about $0.5\%$ of the twins' 2.8-nat entropy gap; the two large
effects sit on the contested arm only (L31\_N11541 $z{=}{+}29.4$, $d_z{=}0.78$; L30\_N1457 $z{=}{+}14.8$)
and are null on the arm where the model actually hedges---answer-competition, not knowledge awareness.
Single neurons register; what registers is two orders of magnitude below what the circuit transfers.
No neuron set moves hedging (77\% vs.\ 0.8\% clean gap; clamps change it by $\leq 2$ points) or verbalized
confidence. In \qw{}: zero frequency neurons, zero specific neurons.

\paragraph{The verdict circuit.}
Figure~\ref{fig:positions}: the entity is read in the first eight layers and the verdict is routed to the
readout positions at L13--19, distributed over 15 verified heads (recovery $>0.05$; largest single-head
recovery L17H20 $=0.39$); in \qw{}'s contested arm the mover heads attend to the conflicting passage (L18H18: 0.28
vs.\ 0.11) and write along the uncertainty direction. Attribution-selected neuron \emph{groups} are
uncertainty-specific where single neurons were not. Jointly patching 20 heads $+$ 100 neurons ($1.95$\% of \lm{}'s heads but $2.55$\% of \qw{}'s---the
sparsity clause fails there---and $0.022$\% of neurons) recovers, with 95\% CIs: familiarity
$0.80\,[0.70, 0.89]$ (reverse $1.07\,[0.96,1.17]$); \lm{} contested $0.89\,[0.69, 1.09]$
($0.78\,[0.65,0.90]$); \qw{} contested $0.86\,[0.77, 0.95]$ ($0.98\,[0.89,1.07]$). Per-pair recoveries
are clipped to $[-1,2]$ and 0--17\% of pairs per cell hit a bound (worst: \lm{}-contested removal), so we
checked the normal-theory intervals against 10k-resample percentile bootstraps; they agree to
$\pm 0.01$ in every cell. Random sets recover $0.02$--$0.04$ (mean over 30 sets per \lm{} cell, 10 in
\qw{}; Figure~\ref{fig:mlp-faith}). This holds in three of four model$\times$arm cells; the fourth (\qw{}
familiarity) has too few gated pairs to run---itself a finding, since the same 1{,}151 candidates gate 195
pairs in \lm{}. Both cross-model cells are the contested arm, where free-generation hedging is $\approx$0
and the log-odds readout is a token-level proxy. Cross-type overlap in \lm{}: heads Jaccard $0.11$ (shared:
L12H28, L15H4, L15H7, L29H0), neurons Jaccard $0.11$, head-recovery Spearman $\rho{=}0.61$---the types
share a variable, not a full circuit.

\paragraph{Decision vs.\ spread: two channels.}
Rank-1 direction patching (swapping only the per-layer uncertainty-direction projection, all positions)
dissects the transfer. \emph{Removing} the contested verdict through the direction alone---swapping the
control twin's projection into the contested prompt---moves \emph{entropy} substantially ($0.57$) while
moving the hedge log-odds not at all ($-0.10\,[-0.32,0.12]$); the full component set removes both ($0.89$
log-odds, $0.33$ entropy), and the direction does suffice for the opposite move: injecting the unknown
projection into the known prompt switches the hedge on at $0.97$. On familiarity the same split is graded
(rank-1: $0.46\,[0.35,0.58]$ log-odds vs.\ set $0.80$), and it replicates cross-model (\qw{}: rank-1
$0.46\,[0.34,0.59]$ vs.\ set $0.86$, entropy $0.84$). The full component set shows the same asymmetry: across all
four \lm{} cells, \emph{removing} the unknown signal (patching the known state into the uncertain prompt)
moves the decision with little of the spread (entropy recovery $-0.13$ familiarity, $+0.33$ contested),
while \emph{injecting} it moves both ($+0.45$, $+0.55$); a paired sign-flip permutation on per-pair
entropy recoveries confirms the direction difference (familiarity $+0.59\,[0.41,0.77]$, $p<10^{-4}$;
contested $+0.22\,[0.06,0.40]$, $p{=}0.015$). \qw{} shows no such asymmetry ($+0.83$/$+0.73$; difference
$-0.10\,[-0.21,0.02]$, $p{=}0.11$), and steering the direction moves both together---so we state it
as a directional asymmetry in one model, not a general dissociation. In the removal direction the mean-difference direction carries only the \emph{spread}; switching the hedge
\emph{off} requires component-specific structure the direction does not capture, even though the direction
alone can switch it \emph{on}.

\paragraph{The verbalization moment, and a prediction that could have failed.}
Decoding every layer through the prefitted Jacobian lens \citep{anthropic2026workspace}---an observational
method sharing nothing with patching---reproduces the causal picture (Figure~\ref{fig:lens}), and decoding
the per-layer uncertainty direction itself, `` unknown'' enters its top-10 at L15 and is its top token
from L17 (`` I'' is present from L13), within the routing window (Figure~\ref{fig:verbalization}); from
L17 the direction's entire top-10 is a hedge lexicon (`` unknown'', `` cannot'', `` unable'',
`` unsure''). Each arm's direction speaks its own vocabulary: the contested direction verbalizes the
\emph{conflict}---`` both''/`` either'' (L11--15) $\to$ `` conflicting''/`` contradictory'' (L16--20)
$\to$ `` ambiguous''/`` inconsistent'' (L21--25), with `` unknown''/`` I'' never appearing---and the
aleatoric direction verbalizes the answer type itself, `` example'' (L14--20) giving way to `` any''
(L21--25). Both vocabularies replicate in \qw{} at L20--24, partly in Chinese; \qw{}'s mid-stack
decodings are noise, so its signal is late-stack only. The framework then makes a
falsifiable prediction, stated before running: the aleatoric arm (spread without hedging) should show a
lens entropy gap with no hedge log-odds gap and no verbalization. In \lm{} it held cleanly (final log-odds
gap $+0.30$ vs.\ $+5.05$ familiarity / $+4.75$ contested; a transient L12--13 blip collapses; entropy gap
$+1.0$); in \qw{} it held only partially (gap $+3.67$, ${\approx}44\%$ of its contested value, emerging
only at L25--26). We report the half-failure as found; a follow-up decided what it is
(\S\ref{sec:undecided}b).

\paragraph{Minimality.}
LOO-ranked greedy pruning saturates in every cell: 20 components reach $0.71$ on familiarity (plateau
$0.85$--$0.89$ by $k{=}60$--$100$), 40 reach $0.91$ on \lm{} contested, 10 reach $0.86$ on \qw{} (plateau
$1.03$--$1.14$)---the earlier non-saturation of attribution-rank-nested sets was an artifact of the
nesting. A sparse circuit of 10--40 components suffices, with the rank-1 direction as its spread
sub-channel.

\section{What our own criteria could not decide}
\label{sec:undecided}
This venue asks what it would take for interpretability claims to fail; three of ours were undecided by our
own tests, and we say so rather than round up.
\textbf{(a) Sparse circuit vs.\ distributed direction --- raised, then decided.} The faithfulness criterion
is met at the point estimate in both \lm{} cells, but the 95\% CI includes values below 0.7 in
\lm{}-contested and touches it in familiarity, and attribution-rank-nested neuron sets did not saturate
(0.23--0.65 at $N{=}10$--$300$). The rank-1 and pruning follow-ups (Results) decided it: a sparse circuit
with a directional sub-channel.
\textbf{(b) Scope of the directional asymmetry --- narrowed by follow-up.} One model, two arms; whether
the asymmetry tracks the model family or the presence of a behavioral hedging repertoire remains
undecided. The \qw{} half-failure of the aleatoric prediction, however, is now decided: at L25--26, where
its $+3.67$ gap lives, hedge tokens hold ${\sim}10^{-6}$--$10^{-5}$ of probability in \emph{both}
twins---there is no hedge decision to read out---and the gap is produced by the generic answer channel
(`` the'', `` that'') deflating ${\sim}100\times$ as mass spreads over the many valid answers (top gained
tokens: `` February'', `` Mars'', `` Africa''), with zero free-generation hedging in either arm (0/390
samples). The half-failure is the spread leaking into the hedge readout where the channels are not kept
apart---the same fact as \qw{}'s missing asymmetry---not a half-verdict
(\path{results/circuit_aleatoric_qwen/decider_*}).
\textbf{(c) The entropy adjustment --- raised, then replaced.} The ANCOVA is unidentified on gated arms
(disjoint supports; signed-shift correlation with baseline entropy $\approx 0$); the random-neuron null
(Results) replaces it with the same qualitative outcome.

\section{Related work}
\citet{teplica2025sciurus} is the closest work: shared uncertainty/factuality circuits across eight models
via causal tracing and zero-ablation. Relative to it we add the gated-twin data standard, twin-based
activation patching (reference-free counterfactuals), faithfulness against size-matched random sets,
single-neuron granularity inside the circuit, the cross-type overlap measurement, and the
decision-vs-spread dissociation---and we inherit its lesson that uncertainty machinery is not modular.
\citet{zhao2026wired} localize confidence-mover components for inflated numeric verbalized confidence (two
models, faithfulness $>85$\%, no random-set baselines); \citet{roy2025unwanted} mask uncertainty heads
driving answer-flipping; \citet{vazhentsev2026heads} use head patterns for hallucination detection;
\citet{basu2025extractiveqa} build context-vs-memory circuits; \citet{arora2026sparse} show task circuits
sparse in the neuron basis (MLP neurons only). On the familiarity arm the closest mechanism is
\citet{ferrando2025iknow}: entity-familiarity directions, found with sparse autoencoders, that causally
steer refusal through downstream attention. Our early-layer entity reading is plausibly the same signal;
what we add on that arm is the verified head$+$neuron circuit it feeds, held-out faithfulness against
random sets, and the on/off asymmetry of its direction. Concurrent 2026 work carves adjacent two-channel
splits---uncertainty vs.\ correctness features \citep{patel2026dissociation}, knowledge vs.\ verbalization
directions \citep{xiros2026knowledge}, and verbal confidence as a cached self-evaluation emerging at
answer-adjacent positions before the verbalization site \citep{kumaran2026verbal}, which our lens timing
complements---and \citet{mazzaccara2026facets} find, at transcoder granularity, uncertainty expressed by a
small set of dedicated features while certainty is a broad default: consistent with our circuit-level
sparsity, and not in tension with our neuron-level negative, which says no dedicated \emph{single unit}
survives an identified null. Directions and features also:
\citet{anthropic2025biology,ji2025hedging}. Neuron taxonomy:
\citet{stolfo2024confidence,gurnee2024universal,du2025posttraining}. Ground truth:
\citet{kadavath2022know,gekhman2024slick}.

\section{Limitations}
Both cross-model circuit cells are contested arms with near-zero free-generation hedging; \qw{}'s
behavioral arm is too small to test; the faithfulness criterion was fixed in code before the held-out runs
but not independently registered; the hedge readout is a token log-odds proxy validated against
free-generation hedging on the familiarity arm only; the ablation reference for gated arms overlaps the
evaluation distribution; the verbalization-rank analysis is \lm{}-familiarity (in \qw{} it appears on a
different token, via steering); two mid-size instruct models.

\section{Conclusion}
A language model's ``I don't know'' is not entropy leaking into words. It is a verdict---computed from the
entity within eight layers, routed by a couple dozen components, and made speakable mid-stack---that exists
before, and in \lm{} moves independently of, the distributional spread that entropy-style confidence
methods measure. Three
practices made this visible, and they are as much the contribution as the circuit: let the model certify
its own uncertain prompts; test every component set against size-matched random ones; and report, in the
paper, which of your own criteria the evidence failed. Anyone probing an internal ``feeling'' in a model
should start with our question: are you reading the decision, or only its shadow in the distribution?
